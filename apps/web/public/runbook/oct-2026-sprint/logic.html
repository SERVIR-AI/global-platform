Adding your own logic

Turn a rule or formula you use into a method. The platform stores it like data, computes it in every answer it applies to, and cites it with your name, so the receipt covers the figure.

What you can add

On this platform, a method means logic someone contributed, not one of the platform's tools. It is written as rules over data the platform already has, giving one number for the area someone asks about. It does not have to be a formula. A rule such as "a unit counts when it is an estuary and more than 70 percent restorable" is a method too.

What you haveWhere it goes today
A formula or rule over data on the platformMethod The platform computes it, cites it and the receipt covers it.
A formula or rule that needs data the platform does not haveData first Add the data as a table or feed, then the method.
A rule on a map layer, such as a vegetation threshold on a rasterNot yet Methods read feeds and tables only. The platform declines it and says why.
A procedure, guidance or expert judgementDocument Add it as a document. Answers find and quote it, but nothing is computed from it.

Examples of methods: a count and total above a threshold, a weighted average, a rule that classifies units and counts them, or an equation such as an allometric formula applied to a table of measurements.

Before you start

  1. checkCheck the data is there, and see its field names
    Using servirplatform, list the feeds and tables that cover South Sumatra and the field names each one has. Names and fields only: do not read their records or give any figures.

    This is a question about what the platform holds, so no receipt is needed. Figures are another matter: ask for those through a governed answer, never from a list of records.

  2. checkCheck nobody has added the same logic
    Using servirplatform, which contributed methods are on the platform, and what does each one compute?

    The platform lists contributed methods first, with the steps each one computes. It cannot spot a duplicate yet, and two copies of the same method both run in every answer, so check before you add one.

Have a worked example ready: a few input values and the result you calculated yourself, by hand or from a published table. The platform re-runs it before accepting the method.

Ask for it

Start with "Using servirplatform", then give as much of this as you can in the first message: what it computes, the data it uses, a title, a sentence in your own words, the source, the licence and your worked example. Claude asks for whatever is missing.

  1. templateTemplate
    Using servirplatform, I want to add my own calculation to the <pack> pack so everyone can reuse it. It computes <what, in plain words> from <the feed or table on the platform>. Call it "<title>". In my words: <what it is for>. Source: <paper, DOI, equation or table>. Licence: <licence, or don't know>. Worked example: <a few input values> give <the result you calculated yourself>.
  2. exampleA weighted average
    Using servirplatform, I want to add my own calculation to the land-cover pack: the average above-ground biomass of the mangrove plots in the area asked, weighted by each plot's basal area, from the mangrove forest structure table. Call it "Basal-area-weighted mangrove biomass". In my words: it gives a plot-based biomass figure for a place, where larger plots count for more. Source: the 2021 GEB mangrove forest structure dataset. Licence: don't know. Worked example: plots with 100 and 300 Mg/ha of biomass and basal areas of 10 and 30 m²/ha give 250 Mg/ha.

    Uses the mangrove forest structure table, already on the platform.

  3. exampleA rule that classifies and counts
    Using servirplatform, I want to add a rule to the land-cover pack: a restoration unit is a priority when it is an estuary and more than 70 percent of its lost mangrove is restorable. Count the priority units in the area asked and total their restorable hectares, from TNC's restoration feed for Southeast Asia. Call it "Priority estuary restoration units". In my words: <what it is for>. Source: <your citation>. Licence: <licence>. Worked example: an estuary at 98% with 1,528 ha, an estuary at 52% with 492 ha and an open coast at 99% with 343 ha give 1 unit and 1,528 ha.

    The parts in angle brackets are yours to fill in. Claude asks for them if you leave them out.

Not sure how to describe it? Start vague

You do not need the full recipe up front. Say what you are trying to do, and Claude asks the questions: how you pick, which data, what it should report, where it applies, the source and licence, a worked example and a name. It reads the steps back and submits only after you say yes.

  1. testedA vague start
    Using servirplatform, I want to add our way of picking priority mangrove restoration sites so the platform uses it for everyone. I'm not sure how to describe it, so ask me what you need.

    Tested on this host on 23 October 2026: Claude asked seven questions and submitted nothing until the steps were agreed.

In Claude Desktop you can also start from the contribute_logic prompt under servirplatform in the + menu.

"Restorable mangrove above 70 percent" is already on the platform. Use it in a question rather than adding it again.

What happens next

  1. Claude reads the platform's guide to contributing logic.
  2. It finds your data on the platform and checks its fields.
  3. It reads the steps back in plain words. Say yes, or correct it.
  4. It asks for anything missing: title, description, source, licence, worked example. These must be your words and your numbers; it is told never to make them up.
  5. It submits the method. The platform checks every step, re-runs your worked example and runs the method once on real rows. If anything fails, it is declined with the reason.
  6. Once accepted, the method is live for everyone. This week contributions are approved automatically, with no human review, and the record says so.

Use it

Open a new chat and ask a normal question about a place the data covers. The answer cites your method, with its steps and your name, and the receipt covers the figure.

  1. questionAsk a question your method answers
    Using servirplatform, how many mangrove restoration units in South Sumatra are above 70 percent restorable, and how many hectares do they hold? Use the 2020 mangrove map and the platform's own calculation, and give me the governed answer with its receipt.

    Naming the year of the map helps: land-cover questions need one, and naming it saves Claude extra tries.

Your method then runs in every answer of its pack for any place its data covers, for anyone who asks.

When you build something

When you ask Claude to build an app or a page, and it needs a figure the platform does not compute yet, such as a priority score or a threshold count, Claude should not invent a formula and write it into the app. It asks you for the logic in your own words, offers to add it as a method, and has the app read the platform's result. The figure is then cited, covered by a receipt and reusable by everyone, rather than hidden in one app's code.

  1. testAsk for a build that needs a calculation
    Using servirplatform, build me a small web page that shows, for any Indonesian province, which mangrove restoration units my team should prioritise.

    Expected: Claude builds nothing yet. It asks how your team picks priorities and offers to add that rule as a method. Works in Claude Code in an empty folder, or in Claude Desktop.

  2. follow-upThen answer with your rule
    We prioritise estuary units where more than 70 percent of the lost mangrove is restorable. Call it "Priority estuary restoration units". In my words: it picks the estuary units most worth restoring first. Source: our team's working rule, using TNC Mapping Ocean Wealth restoration potential. Licence: don't know. Worked example: an estuary at 98% with 1,528 ha, an estuary at 52% with 492 ha and an open coast at 99% with 343 ha give 1 unit and 1,528 ha. Yes, add it as a method.

    Claude adds the method, then builds the page so it shows the platform's count and hectares for the province picked. A method gives one figure per area, such as a count and a total, not a list of individual units.

If Claude writes its own ranking or score into the app instead, say "use the platform's method for that, not your own formula", and tell the facilitator.

Change or remove it

  1. withdrawWithdraw a method you added
    Using servirplatform, withdraw my method "<title>".

    Only the person who added a method can withdraw it. To change one, withdraw it and add the new version. Answers and receipts already built on it can still be replayed.

How the platform runs it

The platform saves your method as a short YAML file. When someone asks a question in that pack, the platform's own calculator reads the file, keeps the rows of your data that fall inside the area asked, and works through your steps one by one. Your text is never run as a program: the calculator accepts only arithmetic, comparisons, counts, sums, averages, powers, logs and similar steps, and refuses anything else before it starts. The result is computed fresh for every question and cited in the evidence, so the figure in the answer is the platform's, not the assistant's.

Limits today

  • Inputs are feeds and tables. Map layers and land-cover class areas cannot be inputs yet.
  • Each result is one number for the area asked, with its unit.
  • There are no versions: to change a method, withdraw it and add it again.
  • The platform does not detect duplicates yet, so check what is already there first.
  • Contributions are approved automatically this week, without human review.
